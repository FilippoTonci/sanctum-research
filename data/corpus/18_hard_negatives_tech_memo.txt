TECHNICAL MEMO - Document automation pilot

Summary
The pilot evaluated three tools for clause extraction: [[?ORGANIZATION|Kira]], [[?ORGANIZATION|Luminance]] and an in-house prototype built on Python 3.12 and spaCy. Accuracy on the CUAD benchmark was 0.81, 0.84 and 0.77 respectively.

Architecture
- Documents are uploaded via SharePoint and converted with [[?ORGANIZATION|Apache]] Tika 2.9.
- The parsing service runs on [[?ORGANIZATION|AWS]] Lambda (eu-west-2) behind an API Gateway; logs go to CloudWatch.
- Models are versioned in MLflow; the current release is v1.4.0-rc2.
- The test server is reachable at localhost:8080 and [[?IP_ADDRESS|127.0.0.1]] only; no public endpoints.

Findings
1. Clause 7.2 (Limitation of Liability) and Schedule 3 (Service Levels) were extracted correctly in 92% of NDAs.
2. The Jaguar and Mercury templates confused the classifier because the product names look like party names.
3. Defined terms such as "the Discloser", "the Recipient" and "Affiliate" should not be redacted.
4. Monetary thresholds (e.g. USD 2,500,000) and percentages are business terms, not personal data.
5. Article 28 GDPR and Section 1798.100 CCPA references are statutory citations, not PII.

Recommendation
Proceed with [[?ORGANIZATION|Luminance]] for the June release; budget GBP 140k for year one. Revisit after the [[?ORGANIZATION|Microsoft]] Copilot integration ships in Q4.
